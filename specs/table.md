# Table spec

## Summary
A flat top held at a working height. Tables are a good second type because they are mostly kit: a slab, legs, and some rails between them. Even so, the five styles read as clearly different silhouettes at grid distance: a long dining table on four legs with an apron, a low and wide coffee table (often with a shelf underneath), a small, tall side table, a bistro café table on one central column, and a farmhouse trestle table with two end frames and a long stretcher. Seeds differ along three axes the eye picks up quickly:
- **Height class:** 0.35 / 0.55 / 0.74 / 1.05 m.
- **Top outline:** rect / round / oval / squircle.
- **Base language:** splayed mid-century legs, turned legs, pedestal, trestle.

Finishes (wood planks, painted, stone, black or metal frames) add the collection feel. Every table also records a seat count (for dining tables), which the wave 4 dining sets can use to place chairs.

## Styles

### dining (weight 0.30)
- Silhouette: a long, high top (0.72–0.76 m) on four legs near the corners, usually with a shallow apron just under the top. Round tops have four legs on a square plan under the circle.
- Parts: top (rect / oval / round / squircle), 4 legs (straight, tapered, splayed or turned), apron rails (85 %), optional stretchers (H or perimeter).

### coffee (weight 0.24)
- Silhouette: low and wide. The height is at most 0.45 of the length, so it reads as "long and low" from any angle. Often has a second slab just above the floor.
- Parts: top (rect / oval / round / squircle), 3–4 legs (straight, tapered, splayed), optional apron (40 %), optional lower shelf (40 %) or stretchers.

### side (weight 0.16)
- Silhouette: small and tall. The height is ≥ 1.0 × the top width, so it reads as a narrow little tower. Round tops on 3 legs are the most common.
- Parts: small top (round / square / squircle), 3–4 slim legs, optional lower shelf (45 %) or perimeter / cross stretchers, rare apron.

### cafe (weight 0.15)
- Silhouette: a single central column on a disc, cross or tripod foot, with a small round or square top. 20 % are bar height (≈ 1.05 m), which gives a tall-stem silhouette.
- Parts: top (round / square / squircle), cap plate under the top, column, base (disc cone / 4-arm cross / 3-arm tripod).

### trestle (weight 0.15)
- Silhouette: a long, thick rectangular top whose ends overhang well past two end frames. Each frame has a floor foot, an upright (I, A or X shape) and a cleat under the top. A long stretcher ties the two frames together.
- Parts: top (rect or soft squircle), 2 × trestle frame (foot, posts, cleat, crossbar for A), 1 long stretcher.

Style weights sum to 1.00. They move weight away from the plan's 0.35 dining / 0.10 trestle because the trestle is the most distinctive silhouette, and dining and coffee share the "slab on legs" language.

## Parameters
Units are meters. "U(a, b)" = uniform. "pick{…}" = weighted pick, with weights summing to 1. All fields are always present in every style. Fields a style does not use get a neutral value (`0`, `false`, `'none'`), following the chair's convention, so the panel and the re-roll tests see a stable field set.

**Draw rule:** each group draws all its random values in a fixed order, unconditionally, and then derives and clamps them. As a result, re-rolling `top` keeps the same leg look (`base` draws the same numbers and only re-fits them to the new top).

## Re-roll groups
| Order | Group | Owns (`fields`) | Reads | Visibly changes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | style (rerollable via `rollStyle`) | style | — | everything except palette; ⟳ steps through the other styles |
| 2 | top | heightClass, topShape, topLength, topDepth, topHeight, topThickness, topEdge, topExponent, topSides, seats | style | outline, size, height, top slab |
| 3 | base | baseKind, legCount, legProfile, legThickness, legSides, legTaper, legSplay, overhang, legHeight, hasApron, apronHeight, apronThickness, stretcher, stretcherHeight, stretcherThickness, hasShelf, shelfHeight, shelfThickness, shelfShape, pedestalBase, baseSpan, baseHeight, columnRadius, columnSides, capRadius, trestleKind, trestleInset, footLength, footHeight, beamWidth, postDepth, cleatHeight, trestleStretcherHeight | style, top | legs, apron, stretchers, shelf, pedestal, trestle frames |
| 4 | palette (`appliesTo`: topFinish painted, or baseMode painted / accent) | paint, accent | — | painted top/base colours only |
| 5 | finish | topFinish, woodName, stoneName, baseMode, topPattern, topPatternScale, topColor, topSecondary, baseColor | style, palette | top material, frame material, planks |

Notes:
- `style` is rerollable through `rollStyle` (ruling R1). Re-roll 1 never returns the base style, so the 22 % repeat rate of a plain re-pick no longer applies. Palette is kept, so the user sees the same paint colours on a different kind of table.
- `base` reads `top`. Re-rolling the top re-fits overhang, splay, apron clamps, base span and trestle inset. Thanks to the draw rule, the leg profile, count and kinds stay the same.
- `finish` reads `palette`, so re-rolling the palette changes `topColor`/`baseColor` (owned by finish), as on the chair. When both top and base are wood or stone, a palette re-roll changes only `paint`/`accent` and nothing visible. `appliesTo` on the palette group (ruling R2) hides its ⟳ for those tables.
- Change-rate check: every rerollable group owns at least one continuous field (topLength, legThickness, paint, the wood/stone jitter in topColor), so the rate should be ≈ 100 %. No `CHANGE_RATE_OVERRIDES` entry is needed.

**How legs meet the floor.** Every floor-touching part is a `strut`, a `puffy`/`beam` box, or a base disc whose bottom face centre is at y = 0. The table uses no `tube` that reaches the floor, so the chair's curvy-leg float (`tests/regressions/chair.json`, tube end centre at y = radius) cannot happen. If a later variant adds tube feet, the tube end centre goes at y = 0, not y = radius, plus a flat pad. Splayed struts tilt their flat bottom cap, which sinks one rim edge by rBottom·sin θ. The splay clamp (≤ 0.14 × legHeight, i.e. θ ≤ 8°) keeps that ≤ 5 mm, well inside `SPLAYED_LEG_FLOOR_SINK` (20 mm).

**legAnchors / buildLegs verdict.** The table can feed the existing helpers for 3–4 leg styles without changes to `src/core`, because the parameter names are generic and the overhang goes into `fp`. It cannot use them for the café pedestal or the trestle, and it needs the leg axis (bottom and top points) to attach apron rails, stretchers and shelves to splayed legs. Hence the requests below. None of them changes `buildLegs` output, so the chair golden test is untouched.

## Grid cell
spacing: 2.3 m, gridSize: 17 (289 items).
- **Driver:** the longest top is 2.0 m (trestle and the dining cap at 1.9). The fits-cell rule needs extent < spacing − 0.1 = 2.2 m, which leaves 0.2 m of headroom plus a gap that reads as "aisle" between long tables.
- **Floor:** the floor is 17 × 2.3 = 39 m across, a bit smaller than the chair's 25 × 1.7 = 42.5 m, so the existing camera framing (`spacing / 1.7` scale) works without new tuning.
- **Budget:** at ~2k tris median, a full grid is ~0.6M tris (worst case 289 × 4k ≈ 1.2M), well below the chair grid's ~1.9M. 289 builds × ~2 ms fits the < 3 s full-grid target.
- **Alternative:** spacing 2.6 / gridSize 15 (the plan's suggestion). It is safer if a later Wave 1 change raises dining to 2.2–2.4 m, but small side tables (0.4 m) look lost in a 2.6 m cell.

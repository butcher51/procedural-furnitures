# Bench spec

## Summary

A bench is a seat for two to four people: a long top on a long-span base. It is fun to generate because the base can vary a lot under the same seat. It can stand on two end frames (cast-iron park ends, slab ends, trestles), four or six legs (staked, tapered, square), or a sled. The top can be gappy slats, one thick plank or a fat cushion, with or without a back and arms. Seeds differ first by silhouette: a park bench's curly ends, a garden bench's high slatted back, a plank bench's splayed sticks, a Nelson-style platform's striped top, or an upholstered bench's soft block. After that they differ by length (1.0 to 1.8 m), leg layout, slat rhythm, finish and fabric. The bench adds two things to the kit: a long-span leg layout (legs in rows, end frames) and a cheap slat primitive. Later types also need both: tables, beds, shelves and sofas.

## Styles

Abbreviations used in the tables below: **Pk** park, **Gd** garden, **Pl** plank, **Pf** platform, **Up** upholstered.

### park (weight 0.24)
- Silhouette: horizontal wooden slats, front to back, on two dark cast-iron or flat-bar end frames inset from the slat ends. The back is three or four horizontal boards leaning back 10–20°. Scrolled arms usually sit on the frames.
- Parts: 4–7 seat slats (run along x) · 2–4 back boards (run along x, on the back line) · 2 end frames, each one side-profile extrusion made of strokes (front leg, seat bearer, rear leg, back upright, optional arm) · variant `scroll` (curled feet and arm ends) or `angular` (straight flat bar, kinked).
- Always has a back. Arms 70 %.

### garden (weight 0.18)
- Silhouette: an all-wood "Lutyens / Scandinavian" garden bench. Square legs are flush with the ends. Aprons sit under 3–6 seat slats. A framed back has many vertical slats (or a few horizontal rails), and flat arms sit on extended front legs.
- Parts: 4 or 6 square legs (the front ones extend up to the arm when it has arms) · front and back aprons, side rails · seat slats (along x) · framed back: 2 tilted posts, top rail, bottom rail, 6–14 vertical slats **or** 2–4 rails · 2 arm boards · optional H-stretcher.
- Always has a back. Arms 80 %.

### plank (weight 0.22)
- Silhouette: one thick, slightly rounded slab, no back. Three bases: `staked` (4 or 6 round legs, splayed both ways, inset from the ends), `slab` (two board ends with a V or arch notch at the foot, like a six-board bench) or `trestle` (an I-post on a floor runner at each end, tied by a long stretcher).
- Parts: slab seat (superellipse outline, exponent 6–10) · base by `support`: staked legs (+ optional short end stretchers) / slab ends (+ optional stretcher) / trestle posts, runners, top cleats and stretcher.
- Never has a back or arms.

### platform (weight 0.14)
- Silhouette: a low, gappy, striped top. Many narrow slats run front to back (crosswise), with visible gaps, on two hidden rails and straight tapered legs. This is the George Nelson platform bench. It is lower and wider than the others and reads as "stripes on sticks".
- Parts: 12–30 crosswise slats · 2 long rails under the slats (along x) · 4 or 6 tapered legs under the rails.
- Never has a back or arms.

### upholstered (weight 0.22)
- Silhouette: a soft cushion block on a plinth or apron, raised on short tapered legs or a sled O-frame. A low upright banquette back appears 35 % of the time. It has no arms, which keeps it apart from the sofa.
- Parts: base box (body) · seat cushion (surface, patterned) · base by `support`: 4 or 6 short legs, or 2 O-frames (sled) · optional back: thin back cushion on 2 hidden posts at the rear of the base.
- Back 35 %. Never has arms.

Readability at grid distance: the frame ends of park, the tall slatted back of garden, the thick slab and splay of plank, the stripes of platform and the soft block of upholstered must each be identifiable in grey. Colour must not be what tells them apart.

## Re-roll groups

Modern grouped RNG: each group draws from `${itemSeed}/${groupId}/${rerolls[groupId] ?? 0}`. The item seed is `${seed}-bench-${n}`. `itemSeed` and `legacyRng` are omitted.

| Order | Group | Owns (`fields`) | `reads` | Visibly changes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `style` (rerollable via `rollStyle`) | style | — | everything except palette and pattern; ⟳ steps through the other styles |
| 2 | `shape` | seatLength, seatDepth, seatHeight, seatThickness, seatSlatCount, slatGap, slatWidth, seatExponent, puff, hasArms, armHeight, armWidth, hasBack, backStyle, backHeight, backAngle, backThickness, backBarCount, backWidth | style | top size and slat rhythm, back on/off and its height and lean, arms |
| 3 | `base` (label "Base") | support, legCount, legThickness, legSides, legTaper, legSplay, legInset, apronHeight, legHeight, frameWidth, frameThickness, footNotch, hasStretcher, stretcherHeight | style, shape | leg layout (frames vs 4/6 legs), splay, frame profile, stretchers. The top and back stay the same |
| 4 | `palette` (label "Colours") | fabricColor, fabricSecondary, bodyColor, paintColor | — | fabric, plinth and paint colours |
| 5 | `pattern` | fabricPattern, fabricScale, stripeDirection, grainPattern, grainScale | — | fabric pattern and wood grain |
| 6 | `finish` (label "Frame") | seatMaterial, woodName, seatColor, seatSecondary, pattern, patternScale, frameMode, frameColor | style, palette, pattern | wood tone, painted vs wood, frame colour |

Cross-group dependencies that the re-roll isolation test must allow:
- Re-rolling `shape` changes nothing in `base` **fields** except the derived or clamped ones: `legCount` (6-leg eligibility depends on seatLength), `legInset` (clamp ≤ 0.12·seatLength), `legHeight` (derived from seatHeight/seatThickness) and `stretcherHeight` (≤ 0.5·legHeight).
- Re-rolling `palette` changes `finish.seatColor`, `seatSecondary` and `frameColor` (when `match` or painted).
- Re-rolling `pattern` changes `finish.pattern` and `patternScale`.
- Why `base` is its own group: every style has at least two base variants (support pick, 4 vs 6 legs, splay, notch, stretcher), so its re-roll changes fields for ≥ 90 % of seeds. It is also the headline feature: the same top on a different base.
- Why the back is **not** its own group: plank and platform (36 % of seeds) never have a back, so a back re-roll would do nothing for them and break the ≥ 90 % rule.

### Can the bench feed `legAnchors` / `buildLegs` as they are?

- `buildLegs` with `legCount = 2`: yes, as is, for trestles. *(Architect: superseded. Use `buildTrestle` kind `I`, shared with the table, which adds the cleat and stretcher and keeps `buildLegs`' sled branch out of the bench.)*
- `legAnchors` with `legCount = 4`: technically yes, but the superellipse spread puts the legs at about 0.93·hx and 0.93·hz. On a 1.6 m bench that is 5 cm in from the ends and only 1 cm in from the front edge, and `legInset` cannot control it.
- `legAnchors` with `legCount = 6`: **no**. The two middle legs land on z = ±hz while the corner legs land at about ±0.87·hz, so the rows are visibly misaligned.
- Radial splay: on a long footprint the corner legs splay almost only along x, and the middle legs only along z. Staked benches splay mostly front to back.
- So the bench needs the small generalisation listed below. It is backwards compatible: the chair passes neither new key and its golden output does not change.

## Failure modes and guards

| Failure | Guard | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Floating feet from tube ends (the chair curvy bug) | No `tube` touches the floor. Every floor contact is a flat-bottomed primitive: strut cap, `beam` foot, `extrudeProfile` with `strokeShape({ floor: true })` or `endPanelShape`. | grounded: `min.y ∈ [−0.020, 0.002]` |
| Splayed struts sink below the floor | Sink = r_bottom·sin θ with θ = atan(legSplay / legHeight) ≤ 13.3°, so ≤ 6.3 mm (inside the 20 mm tolerance). Every splayed leg in an item has the same splay magnitude, so all of them sink equally. | grounded; stable (contacts are taken within 3 mm of the lowest point) |
| Mixed contact depths give a degenerate floor hull (for example sunk splayed legs plus flat frames) | A layout never mixes splayed legs with flat frames or runners. `trestle` uses `legSplay = 0`. Only staked, platform and upholstered legs splay, and they are the only floor contact in their item. | stable (hull has ≥ 3 points) |
| `extrudeProfile` bevel pushes the outline below y = 0 | `bevelOffset = −bevel` in the helper | grounded |
| Slats float between frames or legs (gaps make separate parts) | Each slat spans both supports: park and garden slats run along x over both end frames or side rails, and platform slats run along z over both rails. Slats overlap the support top by 1 mm (slat bottom = support top − 0.001). | connected (2 mm box expansion) |
| Back boards or back posts miss the frame upright | One function, `backLine(p) → { pivot (y, z), angle }`, drives both the park frame stroke's upright and the board pivot. Boards sit on the front face of the upright with 1 mm overlap. Garden back posts start at the rear legs' tops. | connected; front facing (back z-centre < 0) |
| Garden arm doesn't meet the tilted back post | Arm length is derived: the rear end is at the post's z at `armHeight`, `z = −seatDepth/2 − armHeight·tan(backAngle)` (+ post overlap). | connected |
| Tipping backwards (heavy, leaning back) | Park: the rear foot z sits under the back top, `rearFootZ = min(−seatDepth/2 − 0.02, backTopZ)`. Garden: the bbox centre is at about (seatFront + backTop)/2 ≈ −0.07, inside the rear legs at −seatDepth/2 + inset. Back lean is capped at 20°. | stable |
| Too wide for the cell | `overallX = seatLength + (garden arms ? 2·armWidth : 0) + (staked: max(0, 2·(legSplay·sx − legInset)))`. `seatLength` is clamped so `overallX ≤ 1.95`. | fits cell (< 2.10); sane size |
| Too tall / too deep | `backHeight` is clamped so the top is ≤ 0.93. Depth ≤ 0.48 + 0.45·sin 20° + foot 0.04 ≈ 0.69 (< 0.80). | sane size: maxSize `{ width: 1.95, height: 0.95, depth: 0.80 }` |
| Legs poke through the top | `fp.top = seatHeight − seatThickness` (or `− apronHeight` for platform). Legs end under the top. | intersection check (seat vs legs not listed as joined) |
| Stretcher misses splayed legs | Stretcher ends are interpolated along the leg axis at `stretcherHeight`, and `stretcherHeight ≤ 0.5·legHeight`. | connected |
| Upholstered plinth collapses (legs too tall for the base box) | `legHeight ≤ seatHeight − seatThickness − 0.08` | param contract: `seatHeight − seatThickness − legHeight ≥ 0.08` |
| Triangle budget blown by slats | Slats and bars are `beam`s (about 44 tris). Caps: Pk 7 + 4 bars, Gd 6 + 14 + 6 bars, Pf ≤ 30 slats. Worst cases (architect estimate): Pf ≈ 1.6k, Gd ≈ 1.8k, Pk ≈ 1.8k, Pl ≈ 2.1k, Up ≈ 2.2k. | budget: ≤ 6k tris, ≤ 4 draw calls over 500 seeds |
| Platform reads as plank (gaps too small to see) | `slatGap ≥ 0.012`, slat width ≤ 0.06, ≥ 12 slats | visual review |
| Park bench too short reads as a chair or loveseat | Park `seatLength ≥ 1.20`, and frames are inset ≤ 0.12·L | visual review |
| Upholstered bench reads as a sofa | No arms, back ≤ 0.35 m, `seatDepth ≤ 0.48` (sofa is ≥ 0.55), and only 35 % have a back | visual review |
| Garden back too busy at grid distance | Slat pitch ≥ 0.08 m, ≤ 14 slats | visual review (grid read) |
| Stripe pattern runs across the slats instead of along them | Build chooses the material `direction` per part so that grain runs along each slat's long axis. Check on the snapshot. | visual review |
| Centring | Every part is symmetric in x | type check: bbox centre x within ±2 mm (reuse `centredInX`) |
| Back on the wrong side | The back is built only in the pivot at negative z | type check: if `hasBack`, the mean z of vertices above `seatHeight + 0.02` is < 0 (reuse the chair's `chairBackAtRear` logic, gated on `hasBack`) |
| Arms asymmetric | Arms are mirrored with `sx ∈ {−1, 1}` | type check: if `hasArms`, the vertices above the seat are symmetric in x |

Joined pairs, which the intersection check must allow: slat–frame, slat–rail, slat–apron, board–upright, leg–apron, leg–stretcher, cushion–plinth, back cushion–plinth, cleat–post.

## Grid cell

**spacing: 2.2 m, gridSize: 17** (289 items).

- Max footprint: x 1.95 m (1.80 m seat plus garden arms 2 × 0.07, clamped) × z 0.80 m (worst case about 0.69 m), height 0.95 m.
- The fits-cell limit is `2.2 − 0.1 = 2.10 > 1.95`, which leaves about 15 cm between the longest neighbours along x.
- The z gaps are generous (1.4 m) because cells are square. Ruling R4 keeps square cells for wave 1.
- 17 × 2.2 = 37.4 m across, comparable to the chair grid (25 × 1.7 = 42.5 m) and the table grid (15 × 2.6 = 39 m).
- Bench triangle counts (p50 about 2.5k) are close to the chair's. At 289 items that is about 0.7M tris for the grid, well under the chair grid's ~1.9M.
